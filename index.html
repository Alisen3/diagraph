A medical knowledge graph for patients and clinicians

DiaGraph turns scattered health records into a personal timeline of findings, links each finding to a large, sourced medical graph, and explains it to the patient in plain language. With the patient's consent, clinicians get a ranked differential diagnosis with evidence for and against each candidate.

The problem

Medical information is fragmented, and so is the reasoning around it

Patients

Records they can't connect

A person's history is spread across visits, labs, prescriptions and discharge letters. Patients rarely see why a drug was prescribed, what a lab value says about a known condition, or which follow-up a diagnosis calls for. Web searches and symptom checkers only see what they type in, and often produce generic or alarming answers.

Clinicians

Expertise, but limited time

The relevant history is buried in long records, and diagnosis depends on combining findings and their timing. In appendicitis, pain starts around the navel, moves to the lower right abdomen, and is followed by nausea and fever. A flat list of symptoms loses that sequence.

The solution

One shared core, two role-based views

DiaGraph represents medical knowledge as a typed, weighted graph of diseases, symptoms, lab findings and drugs, and represents each patient as a timeline of dated findings linked to that graph. A consent-based sharing flow connects the two views.

Patient view

Understand your own record

  • Your conditions, medications, test results and symptoms as a compact graph
  • Plain-language explanations where every sentence cites its source
  • A timeline of your findings in order
  • Share with a clinician: scoped, time-limited and revocable at any time
Clinician view

Reason faster with evidence

  • The full patient timeline next to the full graph, with all edge types and weights
  • A ranked differential with findings for and against each candidate
  • Temporal pattern matching against curated typical sequences
  • The next question or test that best separates the leading candidates
By design, the ranked differential exists only in the clinician view. The patient view explains what is already in the record and never presents candidate diagnoses. When a clinician confirms or rules out a finding, the result flows back to the patient with attribution.
How it works

Sourced knowledge, verified extraction, transparent reasoning

A graph built from sources, not generation

The medical graph comes from PrimeKG, the Human Phenotype Ontology and ICD-11, plus curated temporal sequences. Every edge carries its provenance, so anything shown to a user can be traced back to a source.

Type-safe clinical extraction

FHIR resources are mapped directly. Free-text notes go through a language model that extracts findings into one shared schema, keeping the exact text span so every finding traces back to its sentence.

A calibrated decision layer

Jev, a calibrated classifier from TypeSafe AI, makes the many small decisions: is a finding negated, whom it refers to, when it happened, whether a concept link is right, and whether an explanation is faithful to its sources.

Safety & privacy

Built for health data from day one

Consent-based sharing

Patients grant access to a specific clinician, optionally for part of the record, with an expiry date, and can revoke it at any time.

Enforced in the database

Role and consent checks run through Row Level Security, not only in application code. Every clinician read is written to an append-only audit log the patient can inspect.

Synthetic data only

The prototype is developed and evaluated on synthetic Synthea patients. No real patient data is used, and only the minimum needed is sent to any model.
